I  —  the question

How to compute more with less resources?

Modern neural networks are magnificent and profligate. The energy ChatGPT spends in a year answering questions could power roughly 43,204 US homes for that same year.

Scaling a model up to reach superior capability matters. Keeping the bill down matters just as much. That tension is the whole of my work: if intelligence is to be available to everyone, and the planet is to survive supplying it, then the interesting question is not how large a system can be — it is how little it can spend.

One honest answer is drawn alongside. Every mote is a query–key pair of a causal attention mask — a triangle, because a token may only look backwards. Keep scrolling and watch what a streaming head throws away: a few attention sinks, a narrow local window, nothing else. The generated text does not notice. The memory bill falls by an order of magnitude.

The card alongside is that same mask at reading scale, synchronised mote for mote with the one drawn at full size behind it.

Retrieval heads keep everything — they have to go and find the needle. Streaming heads keep only a few attention sinks and a local window: 64 and 256 in the paper, four and five in this small drawing. At a 32k context that is a 98% cut in the mask, and end to end DuoAttention gives 2.1× less memory and 3.0× faster decoding with no measurable loss.
